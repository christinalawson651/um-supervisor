The distinction to set up front

Two different things get called “audit,” and the session goes sideways if they blur together. Lead with the second — that’s what this room is here for — and use the first as the “and here’s the clinical layer on top of it” close.

01

Run of show

Seven segments. Numbered because the order carries the argument: prove the record exists, prove the machine input inside it is accountable, prove it’s watched, prove access is governed, prove it survives the retention period, then open the gap list.

1

Audit Trail

6 min
Audit & Traceability → Audit Trail
  • ~3,800 events across authorizations, CM cases, members, reports, user accounts and configuration.
  • Every event carries actor + access role, timestamp, category, action, entity, field changed with before → after, channel, source IP, session ID, correlation ID, reason code, PHI flag, outcome.
  • Filter channel to Fax / OCR Intake, then API, then System Rule — the trail proves how a request physically arrived, which is the first thing disputed when a provider argues a receipt date.
  • Click a row for full event detail, including previous hash and record hash.
  • Verify chain re-walks every event in timestamp order and reports whether the chain is intact — tamper-evidence as a check anyone in the room can run.
The money filterCategory = Configuration. Rule thresholds, criteria-set versions, letter templates, TAT windows and role entitlements all log before → after with a change ticket. Auto-approvals carry the firing rule and version as their reason code, so any automated determination traces back to the exact rule that produced it.
2

AI Oversight

6 min
Audit & Traceability → AI Oversight
  • Every determination the model touched — what it recommended, at what confidence, on which model version, and what the clinician decided instead. 247 scored · 94 auto-cleared · 153 clinician-reviewed.
  • Confidence calibration is the answer to “what evidence supports their reliability”. A band claiming 0.95 should be right about 95% of the time. The 0.90–1.00 band claims 0.95 and observes 86% — nine points hot, across 85 of 247 determinations.
  • Override reasons carry attribution: model-attributable versus legitimate clinical divergence. The top reason is Criteria not applicable to this presentation — model-attributable, and concentrated in the over-confident band. That is a clinical-content loop, not a reviewer problem.
  • Agreement by clinician is a signal, not a score — a reviewer below the group may be catching what the model misses. Nobody gets a rate below 20 scored determinations.
  • Agreement by procedure names where it breaks down: Behavioral Health PHP and Cardiac Catheterization sit lowest.
  • Confidence display is role-configured on the permission matrix — nurse reviewers see it only after recording their own assessment, appeals reviewers are blinded.
Say it in this orderDecision agreement is 85% against a 90% target and the override rate 17% against a 15% ceiling — both miss, and the calibration table explains why. An aggregate that passed while a band ran nine points hot would be the more dangerous result, which is why the acceptance gate fails on a single over-confident band regardless of the average.
3

User Activity Monitoring

5 min
Audit & Traceability → User Activity Monitoring
  • Sign-ins, off-hours access (outside 07:00–19:00), failed sign-ins, out-of-scope access denied by RBAC, break-the-glass grants, and data exports with row counts.
  • Per-account signals: break-the-glass, external IP, no MFA, repeated failed sign-ins, off-hours pattern, high export volume. Click any account for its own trail.
Point to makeOut-of-scope access denied is the control working. Break-the-glass granted is the control being deliberately overridden — and each one is a reviewable PHI disclosure, not a silent exception.
4

Governance & Access Controls

6 min
Audit & Traceability → Governance & Access Controls
  • Four segregation-of-duty rules, each evaluated against the live trail rather than asserted. A clean rule reads “Control passing across N events in window” — an auditable statement, not a green checkmark.
  • Role → permission matrix — ten permissions across ten access roles. The constraint text is the point: “Yes — assigned caseload” is a materially different control than an unconditional grant, and it’s the distinction an auditor writes up.
  • Account inventory with MFA status and last entitlement review against a 90-day cycle.

6

Retention & Archive

5 min
Audit & Traceability → Retention & Archive
  • The Audit Trail queries the online store. This tab is everything behind it — say so out loud, because “3,824 events” invites “and where is year seven?” and that answer shouldn’t be improvised.
  • 3,824 online · 133,745 archived across 46 sealed quarterly segments · 137,569 total retained, back to 2014-10-01.
  • Retention is per record class, not one number: authorization, appeal, CM and audit records at 10 years (42 CFR §422.504(d)); PHI disclosure accounting at 6 (HIPAA §164.528(a)(1)); configuration changes for the life of every record they governed.
  • Verify archive chain walks the sealed segments and confirms each one’s first hash derives from the previous segment’s last — continuous across the archive boundary and on into the online store.
  • Restore requests from cold storage are tracked with requester, reason and turnaround against a 5-day retrieval SLA.
Run the control, do not describe itPress Dispose on a segment under legal hold and it refuses, naming the hold that stopped it. Then run one that is past retention and not held: it takes an independent countersignature and issues a certificate of destruction carrying the retained terminal hash. That is REQ-16 and REQ-17 closing on screen rather than being asserted.

Clinical audit layer

3 min
UM → Audit & Compliance  ·  CM → Audit & Compliance
  • UM: IRR sample with independent re-determination, discrepancy reason codes, corrective-action lifecycle, regulatory TAT compliance per line of business.
  • CM: documentation file review scored element-by-element, pass rate by care manager, rubric-element findings, second-reviewer IRR on the rubric itself, assessment and care-plan window compliance per program, and live compliance exceptions on member records.
  • Appeals has no audit tab, deliberately. It runs on a small hand-authored set of appeal records rather than a generated pool, so there is nothing to sample or aggregate — the thin placeholder that used to sit there was removed rather than shown.

Questions to put back to Centene

Leave the session with answers to these, not just a demo they liked.

Universe layouts
Which ODAG/CDAG record layouts and versions should we build to, and does Centene supply the reconciliation template?
SIEM ingestion
Push into Centene’s SIEM or pull from ours — and what authentication and signing does their security team require?
Retention and disposition
The schedule holds ten years for authorization, appeal, CM and audit records, six for disclosure accounting. Confirm against Centene’s own schedule and any state overrides — and confirm who signs a certificate of destruction, since that step does not exist yet.
Attestation cadence and owner
Is the 90-day entitlement cycle Centene’s standard, and who signs?
Break-the-glass SLA
What review window is expected between an emergent-access grant and compliance sign-off?
Delegation packet contents
Which artifacts must the oversight packet contain, and at what cadence?
AI governance thresholds
Are 90% decision agreement, ±5 points calibration tolerance and ≤15% override the right lines, or does Centene carry its own? And should adverse determinations be held to a higher bar, given the appeal exposure?
Confidence display
Does Centene have a position on showing scores to reviewers — and does reveal-after-assessment work operationally? It keeps the decision agreement measurement honest but costs a workflow step.
Retrieval SLA
Is five days the right retrieval window for archived periods, and does Centene expect a standing feed rather than request-by-request restore?
05

State these in the room

Volunteering the limits is what makes the rest credible.

  • The hash chain in this build uses a lightweight digest so the mechanism is demonstrable in-browser. Production chain-of-custody is server-side SHA-256 — the property being shown is the same: any alteration changes that record’s hash and every hash after it.
  • Regulatory citations throughout are directional. Commercial PPO and ACA Exchange have no federal care-management clock, so those rows measure accreditation and plan policy, not statute. Exact subsections need Compliance validation before anything goes in front of a surveyor.
  • IRR thresholds (90% agreement) and the CM file-audit pass line (80% of rubric) are org policy choices. NCQA and URAC require a defined, followed methodology — not one universal number.
  • The confidence scores, calibration curve and decision agreement rates are generated demo data, not measurements of a production model. The shape is realistic and the failure it contains is a real one — over-confidence at the top of the range — but no claim is being made about a live scorer's accuracy.
  • Decision agreement is measured against what the clinician decided, and in this build the clinician could see the score. That measures agreement, not independent accuracy. A blinded parallel run is the honest test, and it is section 4.2 of the Model Office plan.
  • The clinical audit layer covers UM and CM. Appeals does not have one yet — extending the same pattern needs an appeals pool built first: decided appeals across levels and lines of business, with acknowledgment and resolution clocks, reviewer credential and independence, and notice-content elements. Say it as roadmap; the pattern is proven twice over.
  • The archive is represented by segment metadata — period, event count, hash range, storage tier, hold status per sealed quarter — not by materialising a decade of individual events. That is how a real tiered store exposes cold data, and it is the honest way to show ten years in a browser. Say it, rather than letting “137,569 retained” imply that many rows sit behind the screen.
  • Demo data is deterministically generated from the UM and CM case pools. Realistic in shape and stable across runs, but it is not Centene data.
